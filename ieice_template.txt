An innovative approach relevant to SI optimization in high-speed serial links, particularly a link working with PAM4 modulation at PCIe 6.0 speeds, is proposed in this paper. The composition of a DNN framework together with an R-L-C differential equalizer optimized by BO and IL is understood as one of the probable quick remedies against laborious traditional simulations. Therefore, in spite of specific novelty discussion regarding the R-L-C equalizer and pre-trained DNNs initial training being more detailed than what has been presented here, there hybrid ML approach bears substantial novelty and utility. Experimental results have revealed improved computational efficiency and key SI metrics that can open up new insights for readers involved with machine learning applications on hardware design activities concerning high-speed digital designs.

In the revised paper, please pay attention to the following points:

1. Clarify the expression of "Experimental Verification" in Section 2.3
(Section 2.3, title and p.5, left column, 1st line in last paragraph)
The Section 2.3's title "Experimental Verification" may give the false impression that physical value measurements using real hardware (e.g., oscilloscopes, network analyzers) were conducted. However, the content of Section 2.3  indicates that the validation is based on simulations (ADS and EM tools). Please either rename the section to such as "Simulation-based Verification".

2. Clarify applicability beyond PAM4
The way used does not look to use any characteristic that is only for the PAM4 signal, like the way multi-level amplitude transitions or its waveforms. The current title and words in this paper used now might make it seem this way is just for PAM4. To stop any wrong ideas, it might be good to say in this paper such that the way to make it better can be used with other modulation format, not just PAM4.

3. Clarify whether the R, L, and C values in Table 1 are approximate values or parameter adjustment ranges
(Table 1 and p.4, left column, last sentense in 1st paragraph)
The values of R, L, and C shown in Table 1 do not appear to be approximate estimates based on physical characteristics. Rather, they seem to represent the parameter adjustment ranges specified in the "Tune" function of ADS during the fitting process of the R-L-C model to the S-parameters of the via structure. Please revise the description to clarify this point explicitly.

4. Clarify the input and output data of DNN1 and DNN2 more explicitly
(Fig. 11, or 1st and 2nd paragraph in Section 4.2)
Please clarify what input and output data are used in DNN1 (based on BO) and DNN2 (based on IL). A simple addition to Fig. 11 or a short description in the corresponding section would help readers better understand how each neural network functions within the optimization process.

5. Add a brief explanation for the use of the ReLU activation function
(p.8, left column, 3rd line in 2nd paragraph)
In this paper, the ReLU activation function is used in the DNNs. While the function is a common choice in ML, briefly mentioning the reason for choosing ReLU over other functions (such as sigmoid or tanh) would improve the clarity of your methodology.

6. Clarify the difference between "Eye height of judgment" and "Eye height of Simulation"
(Table 8 and related text in Section 4.3)
For example, the meaning of the term "judgment" in expressions such as "Eye height of judgment" is not clearly defined. Please clarify whether this refers to the predicted eye height used as the objective function in the DNN+BO/IL optimization processes, and explain how it differs from the actual simulation result ("Eye height of simulation").

7. Clarify the meaning of "Iteration"
(Section 4.3, 1st paragraph and Table 8)
The term "iteration" in Section4.3 appears to correspond to the optimization steps (variables B or N) in the BO/IL algorithms in p.8, rather than to the training epochs of the DNN. Please consider clarifying this explicitly in the text or table caption, to avoid confusion between different levels of iteration used in your method.

8. Clarify "IL" as abbreviation for "Imitation Learning", not "insertion loss"
(p.4, right column, 4th line from the bottom)
"IL" is used in this paper as an abbreviation for "Imitation Learning", but in this sentence, it is apparently used as an abbreviation for "insertion loss". Since "IL" is not used elsewhere to denote "insertion loss", please revise this part for clarity. For example, consider spelling out "insertion loss" here instead of using the abbreviation.

9. Correct typos including capitalization and punctuation
(e.g. Section 4.4, beginning of 1st paragraph)
Some typos are observed in the manuscript. For example, in the beginning of Chapter 4.4, "we verify the feasibility..." should be corrected to "We verify the feasibility...", and "four methods, The superior..." should be "four methods. The superior..." with a period instead of a comma. Please carefully that there may be other similar issues elsewhere.
